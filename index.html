Finn Reese

Finn Reese (b. 2008), a high school senior from the San Francisco Bay Area, is a composer, jazz vibraphonist, choral singer, steel pannist, and rock drummer/vocalist.

Finn’s compositions span classical, jazz, and steel pan. He has a particular affinity for choral music and has also composed for orchestra, big band, steel band, string quartet, saxophone quartet, brass quintet, and percussion trio, among others. He studies classical composition with Michael Kaulkin at San Francisco Conservatory of Music Pre-College.

Finn’s compositions are greatly influenced by his performance experiences. He studies jazz vibraphone with Tommy Kessecker at San Francisco Conservatory of Music Pre-College, where he plays in a small jazz ensemble directed by Terrence Brewer. He also plays vibraphone with Stanford Jazz Miles Ahead Big Band directed by Michael Galisatus.

He has sung with Ragazzi Boys Chorus since second grade, and has toured the world with them, singing in great concert halls in Argentina, Australia, Austria, Canada, Chile, the Czech Republic, Italy, and Germany, with a planned tour to South Africa in summer 2027. With Ragazzi he has sung with the Vienna Boys Choir, performed nationally and regionally at the American Choral Directors Association Conference, performed in regional and state honor choirs, and at the San Francisco Symphony.

Also since second grade he has played in the steel drum band at The Nueva School, performing at SFJAZZ alongside pannists Robert Greenidge, Ray Holman, David Rudder, Duvone Stewart, and eleven-time Grammy-winning vocalist Tony Lindsay of Santana. Along with other members of The Nueva School steel band, Finn plans to visit Trinidad for Carnival in 2027. Finn has composed several pieces for steel band.

He sings in a smaller, student-led TTBB choral ensemble, Steller Singers (named in honor of Steller sea lions, which the group’s profits support), and in his spare time he has been the drummer and lead singer of Beyond California, a rock band he co-founded with two friends in fourth grade.

His percussion work, "Binary Rhythms," was named a finalist in the 2026 ASCAP Foundation Morton Gould Young Composer Awards and a finalist in the 2025 National Young Composers Challenge. He has continued his compositional studies at the Boston Conservatory at Berklee and Interlochen Center for the Arts and is currently composing a new choral work to be premiered by the Ragazzi Boys Chorus on their South Africa tour next summer.
